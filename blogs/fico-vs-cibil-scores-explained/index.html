FICO vs CIBIL Scores Explained: The Ultimate Guide

💳

Master Your Credit

Whether you are applying for a mortgage, a car loan, or a premium credit card, your credit score is the ultimate gatekeeper. It dictates not only whether you get approved but also the interest rate you will pay. Depending on your region, lenders rely on different scoring models. The two most prominent are FICO (USA) and CIBIL (India).

Understanding the FICO Score (USA)

Created by the Fair Isaac Corporation, the FICO score is used by over 90% of top lenders in the United States. It ranges from 300 to 850. The higher the score, the lower the perceived risk to the lender.

How FICO is Calculated

Your FICO score is derived from five specific categories of data found in your credit reports (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion):

  • Payment History (35%): Do you pay your bills on time? Even a single 30-day late payment can cause a massive drop in your score.
  • Amounts Owed / Credit Utilization (30%): How much of your available credit are you using? Keeping your balances below 30% of your total limit is highly recommended.
  • Length of Credit History (15%): How long have your accounts been open? Older accounts show a longer track record of responsible behavior.
  • New Credit (10%): Have you recently applied for a lot of new loans? Multiple hard inquiries in a short time suggest financial distress.
  • Credit Mix (10%): Do you have a healthy mix of revolving credit (credit cards) and installment loans (auto loans, mortgages)?

Understanding the CIBIL Score (India)

In India, the most widely recognized credit bureau is TransUnion CIBIL. The CIBIL score ranges from 300 to 900. Lenders in India generally consider a score above 750 as excellent, making you eligible for the lowest interest rates and pre-approved offers.

How CIBIL is Calculated

While the exact algorithmic weighting is proprietary, the CIBIL score focuses heavily on similar fundamentals:

  • Repayment History (approx. 30%): Consistently paying EMIs and credit card bills on or before the due date.
  • Credit Utilization Ratio (approx. 25%): Relying too heavily on credit cards negatively impacts the score.
  • Credit Type and Duration (approx. 25%): A mix of secured loans (home loans) and unsecured loans (personal loans, credit cards) over a long period.
  • Recent Inquiries (approx. 20%): Frequently applying for new loans or cards signals "credit-hungry" behavior.

Key Differences

While FICO caps out at 850, CIBIL goes up to 900. Additionally, the Indian credit ecosystem places a very heavy emphasis on the ratio of secured vs. unsecured loans. Having too many unsecured personal loans in India can hurt your CIBIL score faster than a similar profile would affect a FICO score in the USA.

Universal Rules for Bulletproof Credit

Regardless of whether a lender is looking at your FICO or your CIBIL, the blueprint for a perfect score is universal:

  1. Set up auto-pay so you never miss a payment deadline.
  2. Keep your credit card utilization strictly under 30% (under 10% for the best possible score).
  3. Never close your oldest credit card, as it anchors the length of your credit history.
  4. Only apply for new credit when absolutely necessary to avoid hard inquiries.
