Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Which Payoff Method is Best?

🏔️

Crush Your Debt

When you are staring down multiple debts—credit cards, student loans, a car note, and medical bills—getting out of the hole can feel impossible. Two incredibly popular frameworks have emerged to help people systematically destroy their debt: The Debt Snowball and the Debt Avalanche. But which one is actually better?

Method 1: The Debt Snowball

Popularized by financial personalities like Dave Ramsey, the Debt Snowball method completely ignores interest rates. Instead, you focus entirely on the balance of the debts.

How it Works:

  1. List all your debts from smallest balance to largest balance.
  2. Pay the minimum monthly payment on all of them.
  3. Throw every extra dollar you have at the smallest debt until it is completely paid off.
  4. Take what you were paying on the first debt and add it to the minimum payment of the second smallest debt.
  5. Repeat until all debts are cleared.

The Pros: Human beings are emotional creatures. Paying off a $500 medical bill in two months provides an immediate dopamine hit and a massive psychological "quick win." This motivation is often what keeps people from giving up on their financial goals.

Method 2: The Debt Avalanche

The Debt Avalanche is the mathematically superior approach. It ignores the total balance and focuses entirely on the interest rates.

How it Works:

  1. List all your debts from highest interest rate to lowest interest rate.
  2. Pay the minimum monthly payment on all of them.
  3. Throw every extra dollar you have at the debt with the highest interest rate.
  4. Once that debt is gone, roll the payments into the debt with the next highest interest rate.

The Pros: Because you are attacking the most "expensive" debt first, you will pay significantly less in total interest over the life of your repayment journey. You will also become completely debt-free a few months faster than the Snowball method.

The Real-World Dilemma

Imagine your smallest debt is a $1,000 student loan at 4%, but you also have a $15,000 credit card balance at 24%. The Avalanche method dictates you attack the $15,000 credit card first. However, it might take you a year just to see the balance drop significantly, which can be highly demoralizing. The Snowball method would tell you to clear the $1,000 loan first just to get a "win" on the board.

The Verdict: Which Should You Choose?

Personal finance is more about behavior than math. If we were all perfectly rational robots, nobody would be in credit card debt in the first place.

  • Choose the Snowball if: You have tried to get out of debt before and failed, you struggle with discipline, or you need immediate gratification to stay motivated.
  • Choose the Avalanche if: You are highly analytical, possess iron-clad discipline, and cannot stomach the thought of paying a single extra cent in interest to the banks.
