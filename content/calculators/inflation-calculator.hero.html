Inflation Calculator — Future Cost and What Today’s Money Buys

Inflation is not a news headline. It is the reason ₹50,000 of groceries will cost more in ten years, and why a 7% FD can still lose spending power. This tool applies a constant annual rate: future cost = amount × (1 + i)^years, and purchasing power is the reverse.

Your household basket may inflate faster than CPI if rent, school fees, or fuel dominate. Use 4%, 6%, and 8% as a range rather than one official print. The calculator does not forecast next year’s CPI; it shows how compounding prices eat a cash pile.

Example: ₹50,000 at 6% for 10 years becomes about ₹89,500 of future cost. The same ₹50,000 left idle feels like roughly ₹27,900 of today’s goods. That gap is why emergency cash and long-term investing should not live in the same account.
