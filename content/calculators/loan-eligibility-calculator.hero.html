Loan Eligibility Calculator — Income, Existing EMIs, and Max Principal

Banks do not sanction the biggest EMI you can “survive.” Many Indian underwriters use a FOIR (fixed obligation to income) cap around 40–50%. This tool takes monthly income, existing EMIs, rate, and tenure, keeps total EMI at 50% of income, then reverse-solves principal.

It is a planning ceiling, not a sanction letter. Lenders also check credit score, job stability, and — for homes — property value (LTV). Credit-card minimums and BNPL sometimes count as obligations even if you do not think of them as EMIs.

Example: ₹80,000 take-home with ₹12,000 already in EMIs leaves ₹28,000 of room at a 50% cap. At 9% for 20 years that room supports a much smaller loan than banner ads imply. If existing EMIs already exceed 50% of income, eligibility here is zero until you repay something.
