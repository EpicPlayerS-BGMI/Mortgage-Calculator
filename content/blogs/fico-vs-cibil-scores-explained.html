FICO vs CIBIL Scores Explained

Credit bureaus · Score literacy

Same idea—different markets. How US FICO scores and Indian CIBIL scores are built, read by lenders, and improved before a mortgage or personal loan application.

One Job, Two Ecosystems

Credit scores summarise how you have handled borrowed money so lenders can price and approve credit faster. FICO is the dominant scoring brand in the United States, built on data from major US bureaus. CIBIL (TransUnion CIBIL) is a leading bureau and score in India, sitting alongside CRIF High Mark, Experian, and Equifax India. The maths philosophies rhyme—payment history, utilisation, age, mix, enquiries—but the score ranges, product markets, and lender overlays differ. A “750” is not a universal passport.

If you move between countries, scores do not travel intact. A strong US FICO does not become a CIBIL number automatically; you must build a local file. UK readers should think in terms of Experian/Equifax/TransUnion UK files and lender scorecards rather than FICO-vs-CIBIL labels, though the improvement habits overlap.

When a better score unlocks a cheaper loan, quantify it with the Mortgage Calculator, Personal Loan Calculator, and Loan Eligibility Calculator.

Treat score building as infrastructure for future borrowing costs, not as a game to maximise a number for social media. A stable 760 with low debt often beats a fragile 800 built on juggling limits you cannot actually afford.

Ranges and “Good” Thresholds Compared

Classic FICO scores often run from 300 to 850. Many US lenders treat 670+ as fair-to-good territory and 740+ as stronger mortgage pricing territory, with overlays by program. CIBIL scores commonly use a 300–900 scale. Indian banks frequently prefer scores around 750+ for smoother unsecured and home-loan pricing, though policies vary and thin files can score differently than thick ones.

What Both Models Reward (and Punish)

On-time payments dominate. A single 30-day late on a loan can outweigh months of perfect card use. Revolving utilisation—balances versus limits—moves scores faster than most people expect. Keeping reported balances low before a hard pull helps in both countries. Older, well-handled accounts generally help; slamming new accounts before a home-loan application generally hurts.

Hard enquiries appear when you apply for credit. Mortgage shopping within a short window is often treated more gently in US scoring; in India, multiple loan enquiries in a short period can still look aggressive. Space non-mortgage applications away from your home-loan window.

Mixed credit—revolving plus instalment—can help when handled well, but forcing an unneeded personal loan solely to “diversify” is usually backwards. Earn mix organically. Likewise, maxing a card to earn points and paying later teaches utilisation models the wrong lesson in the month a lender pulls your file.

Public record items, charge-offs, and settlements weigh heavily where they appear. If you negotiate a settlement, understand how it may continue to report and whether a stronger option exists. Paying a collection can help in some systems and matter less in others; verify current scoring emphasis rather than relying on decade-old forum advice.

Worked Cost Example: Score Band vs Loan Price

US sketch: $280,000 30-year mortgage. At 6.3% (stronger band) P&I ≈ $1,735. At 7.0% (weaker band) P&I ≈ $1,862. Difference ≈ $127/month, roughly $45,000+ over a full term if never refinanced.

India sketch: ₹40 lakh for 20 years. At 8.5%, EMI ≈ ₹34,715. At 9.25%, EMI ≈ ₹36,670. Difference ≈ ₹1,955/month—serious money across two decades. Score work that unlocks a better grid row pays for itself.

Building a File When You Are New

Thin files are a special case. In the US, authorised-user status, secured cards, and on-time instalment credit can help establish history. In India, a credit card, carefully used, or a small loan reported to bureaus builds visibility; remaining invisible by using only cash can block approvals later. Always pay in full when possible—building score via revolving interest is an expensive tuition.

New-to-credit professionals should avoid stacking three cards in one month to chase welcome bonuses right before a home loan. One or two well-managed revolving lines plus on-time EMIs on any existing education or auto loan usually build a cleaner story than a sudden burst of enquiries and limits.

If you were rejected for a card, wait, repair the reason (income documentation, existing obligations, identity mismatches), and reapply selectively. Rapid-fire applications across issuers can dig the hole deeper on both FICO- and CIBIL-style files.

Cross-Border Movers: Starting Over Without Panic

Moving from the US to India or the reverse usually means your old score stays behind. Bring proof of identity consistency, open a local bank relationship, and ask whether any lender offers products for newcomers that report to the local bureau. Keep using on-time behaviour habits even before the number looks impressive—those habits are what recreate the score.

Do not borrow heavily just to “generate history.” A small, manageable reported facility used lightly beats a large limit you cannot control. Pair credit building with cash reserves so a delayed paycheck does not create the very late payment you are trying to avoid.

Reading a Decline Letter Productively

Declines often cite score, debt ratio, employment history, or collateral. Map the reason to a timeline: utilisation fixes can take one or two reporting cycles; FOIR repairs may need loan payoff; employment seasoning may need months. Reapplying weekly without changing inputs mostly adds enquiries.

If the decline cites a bureau issue you do not recognise, pull the report immediately. Fraudulent accounts and mixed files are fixable only after you see them. Document calls and keep copies—future underwriters appreciate a clean paper trail when an old error is still populating elsewhere.

Disputes, Errors, and Name Mismatches

Wrong late payments, duplicate accounts, and mixed files happen. Pull reports, document errors, and dispute through official bureau channels. In India, ensure PAN, address, and name spellings align across banks; mismatches fragment history. In the US, check all three bureaus because lenders may not pull the one you monitor daily.

UK Angle for Global Readers

UK lenders rely on credit files and internal scorecards rather than a single consumer-facing FICO number. Electoral-roll registration, on-time household bills where reported, and low credit utilisation still matter. Soft-search eligibility tools help you shop without stacking hard searches. The FICO-vs-CIBIL comparison is US/India-centric; the behavioural lessons—pay on time, keep revolving balances low, avoid new credit before a mortgage—travel well.

A 60-Day Score Hygiene Checklist

Days 1–7: Pull official reports/scores you can access. List every account, limit, balance, and late. Circle errors. Note statement closing dates for revolving accounts so you know which balances get reported.

Days 8–30: Pay revolving balances down toward under 30% of each limit, ideally under 10% on the cards that report soonest. Automate minimums on everything else so a busy week cannot create a fresh late. File disputes with evidence for clear errors.

Days 31–60: Freeze new applications. Avoid becoming an authorised user on messy accounts. If a collections item is legitimate, understand options and documentation before you pay. Recheck utilisation snapshots. Only then time a mortgage or personal-loan hard pull.

This calendar will not erase a recent charge-off, but it removes self-inflicted wounds—high reported balances, accidental lates, and enquiry clusters—that commonly cheapen your rate unnecessarily.

Business Credit versus Personal Scores

Founders sometimes assume company borrowing is invisible to personal FICO or CIBIL files. Guarantees, personal cards used for business, and overdrafts tied to personal PAN or SSN can still surface. Mixing expenses without repayment discipline damages the same personal score you need for a home loan. Keep business and personal accounts cleanly separated, pay personal guarantees on time, and understand when a lender will pull personal bureau data for a “business” product.

How Lenders Use Scores Alongside Manual Underwriting

A strong score does not override fake income documents or an impossible FOIR/DTI. A mediocre score with deep reserves, stable tenure, and a low LTV can still clear at some lenders. Think of the score as a pricing and risk gate, not the entire story. If you are declined, ask whether the reason was score, affordability, collateral, or documentation—each problem has a different fix timeline.

When you receive multiple offers, compare more than EMI: processing fees, prepayment rules, insurance tie-ins, and floating reset language. A slightly higher rate with freer prepayment can beat a teaser that traps you.

Score Myths That Waste Months

Clear these out before you “optimise”:

  • Checking your own score with a soft tool ruins credit (usually false).
  • Closing every old card always helps (often harms average age and utilisation).
  • A 900 CIBIL or 850 FICO is required for any mortgage (false; many approvals occur below the ceiling).
  • Carrying a card balance builds more score than paying in full (expensive myth).
  • Your free app score is exactly what the lender will use (often false).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CIBIL owned by the same company as some US bureaus?

TransUnion is associated with CIBIL in India, but Indian scoring, reporting rules, and lender use differ from US TransUnion/FICO practice. Do not treat the products as interchangeable.

Which score do Indian banks use if I have multiple bureaus?

It depends on the lender’s policy and pull. Some prefer CIBIL; others use CRIF or more than one. Maintaining clean data across bureaus matters.

How long do late payments stay visible?

Negative items can remain for years depending on local rules and item type. Their impact usually fades with time and clean subsequent history, but waiting it out is not the same as disputing a true error.

Do Gig-economy incomes hurt scores directly?

Scores focus on credit behaviour, not salary type. Underwriting, however, may treat gig income differently for eligibility even when the score is strong.

Should I consolidate loans to raise my score?

Consolidation can help if it lowers utilisation and you do not reopen balances, but a new loan adds an enquiry and instalment. Run the total-interest maths before you reshuffle debt for score cosmetics.
