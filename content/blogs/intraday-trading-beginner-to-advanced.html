Intraday Trading: A Beginner-to-Advanced Playbook

Read this first. This is market literacy, not a buy or sell call, not a strategy you should copy with rent money, and not a promise that you can “go full-time.” Same-day trading is a high-friction skill. A large share of retail day traders lose money after costs. CalcBase does not run a brokerage, sell tips, or manage trades. Fund an emergency reserve and a long-term investing plan before you even open a paper-trading terminal.

What Intraday Trading Actually Is

Intraday trading—also called day trading—means you open and close a position in the same session. In Indian cash equities that usually means buying (or shorting via the intraday product) after the 9:15 a.m. IST open and squaring off before the broker’s auto square-off window, typically around 3:15–3:20 p.m., well before the 3:30 p.m. close. You are not trying to own a business. You are renting a price move for a few minutes or hours and paying a toll every time you pass through the gate.

That toll is the whole game. Brokerage, securities transaction tax, exchange charges, GST, stamp duty, and the bid–ask spread all fire on a round trip. A delivery investor might pay those frictions a few times a year. An active intraday trader can pay them dozens of times a week. If your average win is smaller than your average cost-plus-slippage, you can be “right” on direction and still bleed.

Overnight gap risk is the one thing you mostly opt out of—until you do not square off, until you trade derivatives that settle later, or until you hold a future into the next session. Intraday is not safer than investing. It is a different risk: concentrated, leveraged, and behavioural.

The Uncomfortable Base Rate

Regulators keep repeating a statistic that tip-sellers never put in the thumbnail: a majority of retail clients lose money in high-frequency speculative products. The UK Financial Conduct Authority has long required CFD firms to warn that a large percentage of retail accounts lose. Indian market-structure research and broker risk disclosures tell a similar story for leveraged equity and F&O. You do not beat that base rate by watching one more YouTube setup. You beat it—if at all—by treating trading as a small, capped experiment with written risk limits, not as a second salary.

A professional attitude starts with three refusals. You will not fund trades with next month’s rent. You will not use a personal loan or credit card as “trading capital.” You will not raise size after a winning Tuesday because it felt like skill. Those three rules eliminate most of the blow-ups that look, from the outside, like “the market was unfair.”

Money You May Risk Versus Money You Must Keep

Before order types, do this arithmetic. List three months of essential outflows—rent, groceries, EMIs, school fees, insurance. Park that in cash or a liquid instrument you will not tap for trades. Then list high-interest debt. Paying 36% on a revolving card while hunting a 0.4% intraday move is not a strategy; it is a transfer from your future self to the lender and the broker.

Only surplus after that buffer is a candidate for a trading sleeve, and even then the sleeve should be a small fraction of investable net worth. If your surplus is zero, your curriculum is income and budgeting—not candlesticks. Project what that surplus could become as a boring SIP over ten years with the SIP Calculator before you decide it is “idle.” Opportunity cost is a real cost.

Beginner Layer: Accounts, Orders, and One Session Clock

Open an account only with a regulated intermediary for your country. Complete KYC with your own documents. Enable two-factor authentication. Read the product note for the intraday or margin product you will use—MIS-style products in India, margin day-trading in the US, CFDs or spread bets in the UK—because each one has a different liquidation rule.

Learn four order types until you can explain them without a screenshot: market (you take liquidity now), limit (you name a price and may not fill), stop-loss (you define the invalidation of the idea), and stop-limit (the stop can gap through and leave you unprotected). Beginners lose more to “I thought the stop would definitely fill at my price” than to missing a breakout.

For the first month, trade nothing live. Paper-trade one setup only—for example, a failed opening drive back into the first 15-minute range, or a VWAP reclaim on a liquid index name. If you cannot write the entry, stop, and target in one sentence before the open, you do not have a setup. You have a mood.

India, USA, and UK Rules That Change the Game

India: Cash equity sessions run 9:15 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. IST, with a pre-open call auction before that. Price bands and circuit filters exist so a thin stock cannot travel infinitely in one print. Intraday leverage is a broker facility, not a right; it can be cut on event days. F&O has its own lot sizes, margins, and expiry calendar—do not “graduate” to options selling because a reel said it is passive income. Unregistered Telegram “calls” are a compliance and fraud problem; SEBI registration is the filter, not follower count. Tax treatment of speculative intra-day equity, F&O, and delivery differs and changes—verify current law with a tax professional, not a tip group.

United States: Pattern Day Trader rules on margin accounts generally require a $25,000 minimum equity if you execute four or more day trades in five business days. Cash accounts avoid that label but still settle trades and can restrict rapid reuse of proceeds. Shorting locates, halt rules, and overnight buying-power resets are not trivia. PDT is not a conspiracy against beginners; it is a capital cushion the system demands because day trading without it tends to liquidate accounts.

United Kingdom: Many retail traders meet the market through CFDs or spread bets. The FCA has repeatedly highlighted that a high percentage of retail CFD accounts lose money. Leverage looks like a gift until a 2% move against a 20× position wipes the margin. If you cannot explain overnight financing, margin close-out, and the difference between an ISA stock and a CFD, you are not ready for the product.

Cost Drag: Why a “Tiny” Brokerage Is Not Tiny

Suppose you have ₹1,00,000 of surplus trading capital and you average ten round trips a day, twenty sessions a month. That is 200 round trips. Even if all statutory charges plus brokerage plus typical slippage add up to only ₹80 a trip—an optimistic, illustrative figure, not a quote—you need ₹16,000 of gross edge that month just to stand still. A 1.5% monthly hurdle on ₹1,00,000 is already a serious professional target. Most beginners never measure it.

Now add the silent cost: a one-tick adverse fill on a liquid name, twice a day. That is not in the contract note as a line item called “impatience,” but it is in your P&L. The fix is fewer trades, thicker liquidity, and limit orders when the idea can wait. Activity is not a KPI.

40 (2/day) ₹3,200 ₹6,000 3.2%–6.0%
100 (5/day) ₹8,000 ₹15,000 8%–15%
200 (10/day) ₹16,000 ₹30,000 16%–30%

Read the table as a warning, not a fee schedule. Your broker’s actual contract note will differ. The point is leverage of frequency: more clicks raise the bar faster than more “confidence.”

Risk Maths You Can Run on Paper

Professionals size from the stop, not from the dream. A simple cap: risk 0.25%–1% of trading capital on any one idea. On ₹2,00,000, 0.5% is ₹1,000. If your invalidation is ₹4 away from entry, you may buy 250 shares—not 2,500 because the app offered margin. Leverage that makes the notional larger than you can afford to be wrong about is how accounts go to zero in a week.

Worked sketch (illustrative, not a recommendation): stock at ₹250, stop ₹246, risk ₹4 per share, capital ₹2,00,000, 0.5% risk ₹1,000 → 250 shares, notional ₹62,500. A 1:2 planned target sits near ₹258. If you win, gross about ₹2,000 minus costs. If you lose, about ₹1,000 plus costs. Win four of ten such trades and you are still only modestly positive before the days you break the plan. That is why the plan is the product, not the ticker.

Daily stop: three full losses or a −2% day, whichever comes first, and you shut the terminal. Weekly stop: −4% to −6% of trading capital, then you switch to review-only for the rest of the week. These numbers are examples of process, not magic. Write yours before Monday.

Intermediate Layer: Structure, Not Indicator Soup

Once paper results are boringly consistent for two months—same setup, same risk, a journal you actually fill—add structure, not ten overlays. Opening range: the first 15–30 minutes often set the session’s balance; fading every breakout because “it always comes back” is how you buy the third expansion. VWAP: a volume-weighted average many desks watch as a session fair-value proxy, not a divine line. Prior day high/low and a clearly defined news catalyst (RBI, Fed, earnings, index rebalance) explain more than a secret oscillator.

Liquidity is a feature. Index names and the most active futures usually hurt you less on the spread than a ₹80 story stock with a 40-paise quote. If you cannot exit a full size inside a few ticks, your stop is a wish. Advanced traders still prefer thick books; they just execute cleaner.

News is not a setup. A headline can be the reason volatility exists. Trading the first spike without a pre-written invalidation is how beginners donate to people who already had a view. If you did not have the level on the chart before the print, you are improvising.

Advanced Layer: Expectancy, Drawdowns, and Permission to Stop

Expectancy is average win times win rate minus average loss times loss rate, after costs. Track it in R (multiples of initial risk), not in rupee vanity. A trader who wins 38% of the time with 2.2R winners and 1R losers can be viable. A trader who wins 70% with 0.4R winners and 1.5R losers is a slow-motion liquidation even while the win-rate screenshot looks impressive.

Drawdowns are not a personality test. If your measured max historical paper drawdown is 8% of capital, a live 12% hole means either the sample was too small or you are not following the rules. Cut size in half after a 6% hole; do not “make it back” with double size. Revenge size is how a bad week becomes a career-ending month.

The advanced skill nobody sells is quitting. Quitting a session after the daily stop. Quitting a setup that stopped working after a regime change. Quitting day trading entirely if, after a year of honest journals, expectancy is negative and your sleep is worse. A SIP into a diversified fund is not a consolation prize. For most people it is the higher-probability path. Run that comparison with the Investment Calculator using conservative rates—not the 5% day you remember.

A 90-Day Curriculum Instead of a Hot Streak

Days 1–30: Mechanics only. Platform, order types, contract notes, tax folder. Paper-trade one setup. Screenshot every trade with the reason written before entry. No live money.

Days 31–60: Still paper, but grade process: Did I honor the stop? Did I add size after a loss? Did I trade the first ten minutes without a plan? Score process, not P&L. If process scores are poor, do not “go live to feel it.” The feeling is the bug.

Days 61–90: If—and only if—rules were followed and expectancy is not a disaster, go live at one-quarter of intended size, still 0.25%–0.5% risk, still a daily stop. You are not a professional at day 90. You are a beginner with a logbook. That is the honest promotion.

Intraday Mistakes That Look Like Ambition

These are the leaks that empty accounts while the trader still believes they are “learning fast”:

  • Turning a losing intraday long into delivery “because it will come back”—that is a new strategy invented in pain.
  • Averaging a loser with extra margin so the average looks closer to the last print.
  • Copying a call from an unregistered advisor and calling it a system.
  • Trading illiquid small-caps with MIS leverage because the percentage move looks exciting.
  • Skipping the journal on red days, which is when the journal is most valuable.
  • Measuring skill over 12 trades instead of 100, then raising size.
  • Using F&O before you can explain margin, expiry, and assignment risk in plain language.

What a Decent Journal Looks Like

Minimum fields: date, symbol, setup name, planned entry/stop/target, actual fills, size, R result, costs, screenshot, one sentence on emotion, one sentence on whether you followed the plan. Weekly: win rate, average R, biggest rule-break, hours slept, whether you traded after a stressful non-market event. If you will not keep this, you are gambling with extra steps. Gambling can be entertainment if sized like entertainment. It is not a career plan.

A trading sleeve that cannot survive a −20% month should not exist. Compare that sleeve with a decade of systematic investing and with inflation on idle cash. Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need to start intraday trading?

Brokers may let you start with a small surplus, but a tiny account plus leverage is how a few bad fills become a 30% drawdown. Size from a 0.25%–1% risk cap after emergency savings exist. If that implies laughably small share counts, that is the market telling you to paper-trade longer—not to borrow.

Is intraday trading the same as investing?

No. Investing buys cash flows and time. Intraday rents a short price discrepancy and pays costs on every round trip. You can do both, but mixing them mid-trade—turning a day trade into a long-term hold—is how theses get invented after the stop was hit.

Should beginners trade options or futures first?

Usually no. Derivatives add expiry, lot size, and margin mechanics on top of direction risk. Learn cash-market execution, stops, and journaling first. Selling options for “premium income” is not a beginner module.

Do indicators like RSI guarantee wins?

No. Oscillators describe recent price. They do not know your costs, your size, or the next tick. A written invalidation and a liquid name matter more than a colourful overlay.

Can I quit my job after three green months?

Three months is noise. Professionals think in years of expectancy, worst-case drawdown, and living costs that do not depend on next week’s tape. Treat early profits as a sample, not a salary.

What if I just copy a paid signal group?

Then you do not have a process, you have a dependency—and often an unregistered advisory relationship. If the group disappears, so does your “edge.” Learn to say no.
